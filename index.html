How it works

Both tyres sit on flat ground. The front axle hangs off the steering axis by the fork length below the bottom yoke, minus what the fork has compressed, and sits forward of the axis by the offset. The rear axle comes from the swingarm angle, which the linkage and shock set. The tool tilts the whole frame until both tyres touch the ground, then measures everything in that position.

Sliding the tubes up through the yokes and fitting spacers both shorten the fork. A spacer also takes the same amount off the travel. Sliding the tubes doesn't, but it eats into the gap between the tyre and the bottom yoke, so watch that number at full compression.

Sag is where the springs hold up the sprung load. On the fork that's the load shared between two springs, less the preload. At the rear it goes through the linkage, as in the Linkage Bench. Air in the forks, damping and the rider's weight shifting under braking aren't modelled.

Trail is measured on the ground, from where the steering axis meets it back to the front contact patch. Arm angle is the swingarm against the ground, the way you quote it.
